Why do people queue for things they do not really want?

The simple answer is scarcity. If a line is long, we assume there must be something valuable at the end of it.

But the stranger answer is social proof. A queue turns private uncertainty into public evidence. You do not need to know whether the ramen is good. The line tells you other people think it might be.

Then sunk cost joins in. Once you have waited fifteen minutes, leaving feels like admitting the first fifteen minutes were wasted.

So the queue becomes a tiny economy of hope, status and embarrassment.

Sometimes the product is the thing at the counter.

Sometimes the product is the feeling that you spotted value before everyone else did.
