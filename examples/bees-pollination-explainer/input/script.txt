Before flowers invented pollination, were bees just unemployed?

Were millions of bees sitting around on the dole, waiting for plants to create jobs?

Then a flower appears:

"Right, lads. We need pollen delivered."

And the bees put on tiny high-vis jackets.

Obviously not.

Pollination came before bees.

Long before flowers, seed plants threw pollen into the wind and hoped it landed somewhere useful. It worked, but it was wildly inefficient.

Then insects crawled over plants looking for food. Pollen stuck to their bodies and travelled with them.

An accidental delivery service - much better than wind.

Plants that attracted insects reproduced more. So over millions of years, they evolved brighter colours, stronger smells and nectar.

Plants invented advertising.

Bees came later. They evolved from predatory wasps that stopped hunting insects and began collecting pollen and nectar.

They weren't unemployed. They had jobs.

They were wasps.

They just made one enormous career change: from hunting insects to flower logistics.

Which brings us back to the ridiculous question...

Before flowers invented pollination, were bees just unemployed?
